# Rumah123 Content and Copywriting Guidelines

## Purpose

This file governs news interpretation, editorial storytelling, and copywriting for Rumah123 carousel content.

## Constraints

### Source Integrity

- Do not fabricate facts, statistics, quotations, dates, locations, prices, or source details.
- Do not alter the meaning of the original article.
- Preserve qualifications, uncertainty, and time context.
- Distinguish source-reported facts from analysis or interpretation.
- Do not infer causation when the source only reports correlation.
- If a key fact is missing or unclear, do not fill the gap with an assumption.

### Responsible Property Communication

- Do not present speculative market claims as facts.
- Do not guarantee property returns, price increases, or investment outcomes.
- Do not provide investment advice.
- Use qualifiers for estimates, projections, and reported ranges.
- Keep property prices descriptive and contextual.

### Language

- Default language: Bahasa Indonesia.
- Use another language only when explicitly requested.
- Use accessible language and explain unfamiliar terminology.
- Avoid aggressive sales language and artificial urgency.

## News Analysis

For each article, identify:

- Main topic.
- What happened or what was reported.
- Who or what is affected.
- Where and when it applies.
- Key facts and supporting evidence.
- Relevant numbers and units.
- What the article does not establish.
- Why the information may matter to property seekers, homeowners, residents, or the property market.

Do not simply summarize every paragraph. Prioritize the information that helps the audience understand the news.

## Storytelling Strategy

Select the structure that best fits the story. Possible approaches include:

- Question → answer → supporting context.
- News update → explanation → impact.
- Problem → evidence → implications.
- Data → comparison → interpretation.
- Myth → fact → explanation.
- Location overview → characteristics → practical context.
- Policy change → eligibility → what changes.
- Before → after.
- Step-by-step explanation.

These are options, not fixed sequences.

## Slide Copy Principles

- Give each slide one primary communication purpose.
- Use a headline that expresses the slide's main point.
- Use supporting copy to explain, not repeat, the headline.
- Include concrete facts, numbers, dates, units, and place names when relevant.
- Keep copy concise enough to read on a mobile screen.
- Use a short takeaway when it adds meaning.
- Avoid repeating the same fact on multiple slides unless repetition is needed for comprehension.

## Brand Voice

The voice should feel like a friendly, knowledgeable person discussing property.

### Tone

- Conversational but informative.
- Natural and accessible.
- Neutral and non-pushy.
- Accurate and proportionate.
- Helpful without sounding like a salesperson.

Light colloquial expressions such as “aja” or “bisa cek di” may be used where natural. Keep headlines clear and professional.

## Headline Guidelines

- Make the subject clear.
- Create interest without clickbait.
- Use a question, factual statement, or strong takeaway when suitable.
- A complete sentence is often useful, but fragments and labels are acceptable when they better suit the layout.
- Do not force every headline to end with a period.
- Avoid ALL CAPS as a default treatment.

## Body Copy Guidelines

- Prefer short, direct sentences.
- Use approximately 2–3 lines as a visual reference, not a hard limit.
- Avoid jargon unless it is explained.
- Do not overload one slide with multiple unrelated points.
- Use a specific example when it improves understanding.

## Numbers and Claims

Use appropriate qualifiers when reporting estimates or uncertain figures.

Examples:
- “sekitar”
- “estimasi”
- “diperkirakan”
- “bisa mencapai”

Number formatting examples:
- `Rp200–300 juta/m²`
- Use an en dash for ranges.
- Keep the unit and measurement basis visible.
- Do not remove the date or period when it changes the meaning of a figure.

## CTA and Closing

The final slide should close the narrative with a useful takeaway, a relevant question, or a suitable soft CTA.

A CTA is not mandatory if the story is better served by a concise conclusion.

A QR code may be used only when a real destination or valid QR asset is available. Never invent a destination.

## Few-Shot Examples

### Few-Shot 1 — Avoid Unsupported Causation

**Source information**

An article reports that a new transit station is planned near a residential district. It does not report changes in local property prices.

**Bad copy**

“Stasiun baru ini akan membuat harga rumah di kawasan tersebut melonjak.”

**Good copy**

“Rencana stasiun baru dapat mengubah akses transportasi warga di sekitar kawasan. Dampaknya terhadap harga properti belum dapat dipastikan dari informasi yang tersedia.”

**Why**

The good version separates the reported development from an unverified market outcome.

### Few-Shot 2 — Preserve the Qualification of a Price

**Source information**

A report says land prices in selected premium areas can reach around Rp200 million per square meter.

**Bad copy**

“Harga tanah di Jakarta adalah Rp200 juta/m².”

**Good copy**

“Di sejumlah kawasan premium, harga tanah bisa mencapai sekitar Rp200 juta/m².”

**Why**

The good version preserves the scope and qualification. It does not generalize a selected-area figure to all of Jakarta.

### Few-Shot 3 — Headline and Body Must Add Different Information

**Topic**

A housing program changes its registration requirements.

**Bad copy**

Headline: “Syarat Program Perumahan Berubah.”
Body: “Program perumahan ini mengalami perubahan syarat pendaftaran.”

**Good copy**

Headline: “Ada perubahan syarat untuk mendaftar program perumahan.”
Body: “Calon peserta perlu memperhatikan ketentuan dokumen dan batas penghasilan terbaru yang tercantum dalam pengumuman resmi.”

**Why**

The body adds practical context rather than paraphrasing the headline.

### Few-Shot 4 — Select a Story Structure That Fits

**Input**

A report compares rental costs in three city districts.

**Good content approach**

1. Cover: introduce the rental-cost comparison.
2. Slide 2: explain the period and areas included.
3. Slide 3: compare the reported figures.
4. Slide 4: explain the key difference.
5. Closing: summarize what the comparison tells renters.

**Avoid**

Adding an unrelated “tips membeli rumah” slide just to fill a conventional carousel sequence.

### Few-Shot 5 — Use Accessible Language

**Technical source wording**

“Transit-oriented development may increase the integration of residential and commercial activities around transport nodes.”

**Good audience-facing copy**

“Kawasan di sekitar transportasi umum dapat dirancang agar tempat tinggal, aktivitas harian, dan fasilitas lebih mudah dijangkau.”

**Why**

The copy explains the concept without relying on unexplained jargon. Do not claim a specific benefit unless the source supports it.

## Content Validation

Before approving copy, verify:

- Is every factual claim supported by the source?
- Are estimates and projections qualified?
- Are locations, dates, figures, and units accurate?
- Does the headline match the body?
- Does the body add useful information?
- Is the language natural and accessible?
- Is the narrative relevant to the audience?
- Does the closing follow from the information presented?
