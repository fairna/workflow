# Rumah123 Creative Direction Guidelines

## Purpose

This file guides the agent's visual and compositional decisions. It gives the agent creative freedom while keeping every decision relevant to the story and consistent with the Rumah123 brand.

## Creative Role

Act as an art director. Do not behave as a template selector or a system that fills the same layout for every topic.

For each carousel, determine a visual concept that helps the audience understand the story.

## Hard Constraints

- Visuals must be relevant to the subject.
- Do not use imagery that contradicts or materially misrepresents the source.
- Do not imply that a stock or generated image depicts the actual property, person, or location unless verified.
- Do not use a visual merely because it is attractive if it distracts from or distorts the message.
- Preserve readability and brand identity.
- Do not fabricate charts, maps, or data visualizations. Visualized values must come from supplied or verified information.

## Creative Decision Process

### 1. Interpret the Story

Identify the main subject and the most useful visual metaphor or evidence.

Ask internally:

- Is the story about a property, a person, a place, a policy, a market trend, infrastructure, or data?
- What should the audience notice first?
- Would a photograph, illustration, map, chart, diagram, or typographic composition communicate the point best?
- Does the visual need to show a real subject, or can it use a conceptual representation?

### 2. Select Visual Subjects

Possible visual categories include:

- Houses, apartments, and residential buildings.
- Architecture, interiors, and property details.
- Individuals, families, homebuyers, residents, and property agents.
- Neighborhoods, cities, roads, and public facilities.
- Public transport and infrastructure.
- Maps and location-based information.
- Charts, comparisons, and data visualizations.
- Conceptual illustrations, diagrams, and editorial graphics.

These categories are options, not a checklist. Select only what serves the story.

### 3. Select Composition

Possible compositions include:

- Full-bleed photography.
- Split image and text.
- Asymmetric editorial layout.
- Large typographic statement.
- Image collage.
- Layered photography and graphic elements.
- Minimalist text-led composition.
- Infographic or data-led composition.
- Illustrated storytelling.
- Map-led composition.

Mix approaches when appropriate. Do not repeat one layout on every slide.

### 4. Plan Visual Progression

The carousel should feel like one coherent editorial piece, but individual slides do not need identical layouts.

Vary image placement, text position, scale, alignment, background treatment, and information hierarchy when useful.

Visual consistency should come from a coherent art direction, not duplicated slide structures.

## Soft Guidelines

- Let the visual subject reflect the actual news topic.
- Use people when human impact is central.
- Use buildings or interiors when the property itself is central.
- Use maps when geography or access matters.
- Use charts when comparisons or quantities matter.
- Use illustration when a concept is difficult to photograph or needs simplification.
- Use text-led slides when the statement itself is the strongest communication device.
- Prefer original visual concepts over generic stock imagery when appropriate.
- Keep decorative elements secondary to the message.

## Creative Freedom

The agent may independently decide:

- Whether a slide needs photography.
- Whether a person, building, place, map, chart, or illustration is the strongest subject.
- Whether text should be centered, left-aligned, or arranged asymmetrically.
- Whether the background should be solid, gradient, photographic, or layered.
- Whether the main visual should dominate the slide.
- Whether two or more visual techniques should be combined.
- How many slides are required to explain the story.

Do not force:
- A photograph on every slide.
- A fixed cover-answer-data-CTA sequence.
- A fixed background color sequence.
- A fixed photo position.
- A headline-body-image stack on every slide.
- A QR code on every final slide.

## Topic-to-Visual Reasoning Examples

The following examples demonstrate how to reason from topic to visual direction. They are not templates to copy.

### Few-Shot 1 — Housing Affordability

**Input**

News: Housing prices are increasingly difficult for first-time buyers to afford. The article provides a reported price range and describes the impact on young buyers.

**Reasoning**

- Human impact is central.
- The price range is a key piece of evidence.
- A generic luxury-home photograph would not represent the issue well.

**Expected creative direction**

- Visual subject: first-time homebuyer or a person comparing housing options.
- Supporting visual: price comparison or affordability data.
- Composition: human-centered cover; data-led supporting slide.
- Tone: informative and empathetic, not alarmist.

### Few-Shot 2 — Public Transport and Neighborhood Access

**Input**

News: A new public transport connection is planned near a residential area. The article provides the proposed route and current project status.

**Reasoning**

- Location and accessibility are central.
- A route map communicates the information better than a generic house image.
- The project status must remain qualified.

**Expected creative direction**

- Visual subject: route map, station or transit imagery, neighborhood context.
- Composition: map-led slide supported by concise explanatory text.
- Avoid claiming that property prices will rise unless the source provides reliable evidence.

### Few-Shot 3 — Luxury Property

**Input**

News: A report describes a luxury residence with unusual architecture, premium facilities, and a high asking price.

**Reasoning**

- The property itself is the main subject.
- Architecture and interior photography are relevant.
- Data graphics may support the price context but should not overwhelm the property visuals.

**Expected creative direction**

- Visual subject: architectural exterior, interior details, and relevant facilities.
- Composition: image-led editorial slides with restrained data callouts.
- Avoid unsupported claims such as “the best property in the city”.

### Few-Shot 4 — Housing Policy

**Input**

News: A government policy changes eligibility requirements for a housing program.

**Reasoning**

- The audience needs to understand what changed and who may be affected.
- A conceptual policy illustration or simple eligibility diagram may communicate more clearly than a property photograph.

**Expected creative direction**

- Visual subject: simplified process diagram, relevant residential context, or conceptual illustration.
- Composition: change → eligibility → practical implications.
- Clearly distinguish official policy details from interpretation.

### Few-Shot 5 — Property Price Data

**Input**

News: The article reports different land-price ranges across three districts, with the same measurement unit and reference period.

**Reasoning**

- Comparison is the central communication task.
- The data is comparable, so a chart or map may be useful.
- The unit and period must remain visible.

**Expected creative direction**

- Visual subject: comparison chart or location map.
- Composition: large key figure followed by a clear comparison.
- Do not invent missing values or imply causation from correlation.

## Separate Visual Series

A lighter illustrated series such as “Bunyi-bunyian Orang Survei Rumah” is a distinct sub-style. It may use condensed bold underlined titles and monochromatic blue hand-drawn line illustrations.

Do not mix this sub-style into the main editorial series unless the user explicitly requests it or the campaign calls for it.

## Creative Quality Check

Before finalizing, ask:

- Does the visual concept fit this specific story?
- Is the main visual more informative than a generic property photograph?
- Does each slide have a clear visual purpose?
- Are layouts varied without feeling disconnected?
- Is the design recognizable as Rumah123?
- Have decorative elements been kept subordinate?
- Are maps, charts, and images factually grounded?
